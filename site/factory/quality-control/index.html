Pre-Production Quality Planning

Materials and Sample Approval

Clear standards before production help prevent costly mistakes during mass production.

  • Material and component inspection
  • Approved sample confirmation
  • Product specification review
  • Pantone colour and artwork approval
  • Packaging and labelling confirmation
  • Testing requirements confirmation

Incoming material is not taken on trust. Resin grade and supplier lot are checked against the approved specification before the bag is opened, silicone hardness is checked against the approved sample, and colour is matched to the chip under the same light source used for approval. The approved sample, artwork version and dimension sheet are then pulled and attached to the order — that set is the acceptance reference for every later check, including a repeat order two years later. The first parts off the press are measured and kept: dimensions, capacity, wall thickness and lid fit are compared with the drawing before the run continues.

During Manufacturing

In-Process Quality Control

Our QC team checks key production steps to catch problems early. We inspect:

  • Product size and structure
  • Rim and stem strength
  • Surface finish and colour
  • Logo quality and position
  • Plastic-to-silicone part fit

Inspectors pull units through each shift and check appearance, flash, sink marks, print position and assembly. A drifting parameter is corrected at the machine rather than sorted out at the end of the run — which is why an in-process check catches what a final inspection can only reject.

After Manufacturing

Final Inspection Before Shipment

Finished products are checked against the approved sample and order requirements. We inspect:

  • Seal and leakage
  • Appearance and surface finish
  • Logo quality and position
  • Quantity and accessories
  • Barcodes, packaging and cartons

Before the carton is sealed, the batch is sampled by size, opened, measured and leak-tested where the order calls for it. Carton weight, drop test and shipping marks are checked on the packed carton — the failures that only show up after a sea freight, not on the line.

How many units we open — set by batch size

AQL sampling is a statistical check, not a promise of zero defects. What it does promise is that a defect rate above the agreed level is caught here rather than in your warehouse.

Under 500 pcs — 5%

Appearance, dimensions and assembly checked on every opened unit.

500 – 2,000 pcs — 3%

The above, plus a water leak test on assembled units.

Over 2,000 pcs — 1%

The above, plus carton drop test and shipping marks on the packed carton.

The plan is on the quotation

The sampling plan and the tests are stated on the quotation, so the inspection you receive is the inspection you agreed to. Where a buyer has their own AQL level or a third-party inspection booked, the plan follows their standard instead.

These levels follow ISO 2859-1, a paid standard published by ISO. We do not resell it or summarise it here — buy it from the issuing body if you want to read the clause before accepting our word for it.

Questions buyers ask before an inspection

Can I book a third-party inspection?

Yes, and we encourage it on a first order. Tell us the agency and the date; we schedule production so the goods are packed and accessible when the inspector arrives. The inspection fee is yours, the access is ours.

What happens if the inspection fails?

The batch is sorted against the agreed standard before shipment, and the re-inspection is at our cost when the failure is ours. If a specification was ambiguous, we fix the specification first — otherwise the same failure returns on the next run.

Do you keep a sample of my order?

Yes. One approved sample per version stays with the order record. If you need a duplicate sent to your office, ask at quotation.

Is a test report included in the price?

Not by default. Reports are issued per model by a third-party lab and charged at cost; they are quoted as a separate line so you can decide whether your market needs one.
