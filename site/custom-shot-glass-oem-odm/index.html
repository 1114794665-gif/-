Route 01

Existing mold manufacturing

A tool that already exists is the cheapest and fastest route, and the one most shot glass projects should start with. It is also the route where we most often tell a buyer that a new mold is not needed.

Choose it when
The capacity and the shape already work, and what has to change is the colour, the logo or the packing.
What you send
The reference model or a sample, the logo, the quantity and the pack plan.
What we check
Cavity condition and wear, gate and ejection marks, whether the tool still holds the recorded dimensions.
What you cannot change
Capacity, wall section, height and base geometry — those are the tool.

Route 02

Existing mold modification

Most “new product” requests are a change to one surface of an existing tool: a different capacity, a taller rim, a heavier base, a flat panel for the logo. That is an insert change, not a new mold.

Choose it when
The glass is close to right but the pour, the rim or the base is wrong.
What changes
Capacity by insert, rim profile, base geometry, wall section, a flat print area.
What we re-check
Shrinkage, ejection, tolerance stack-up against the unchanged parts, and whether the new insert fits the existing frame.
The honest limit
If the change needs a different frame or more than one insert, it is priced as a new tool and we say so before quoting.

Route 03

New mold development

A glass that does not exist yet. Two to four tool sets depending on the design, a dimensional review before cutting, trial rounds until the sample is right, then production on the approved tool.

  • Tooling quoted as its own line: sets, cavity count, steel, slides, trial rounds
  • DFM report before the quote, so cost is still negotiable
  • T1 samples measured against the drawing, not against a photo
  • Production minimum stated with the tooling, because the tool is paid for by the run
  • Tooling ownership written into the project agreement

If your drawing can be made on an existing tool, we will say that instead of quoting a new one. Quoting a new mold for an existing shape is the fastest way to lose a repeat account.

What a tool actually is

A shot glass mold is two halves that have to agree: the cavity block that shapes the outside, and the cores that shape the inside. Wall section, base thickness and capacity all fall out of that pair, which is why none of them can be quoted from a photo.

  • Cavity count sets cycle time and tooling cost, so it is quoted as its own line
  • Steel grade and slide count are stated before the price, not after the sample
  • The print band is checked on the tool drawing, before any steel is cut
  • Every trial round is measured and reported, not just photographed

Logo and packaging

Decoration is decided with the tool, not after it

Same building · same project

A logo sits on a surface that the mold has already decided. On a shot glass the print band is a narrow strip between the taper and the base radius, so the method, the number of colours and the print position are agreed while the tool is still being drawn — not after the first carton arrives.

Print method

Screen, UV, pad, foil

Four methods plus a frost or etch-look texture carried by the tool itself. The method follows the artwork: solid spot colours, gradients, small curved marks and metallic accents each point to a different process.

What you approve

The proof, on the real taper

A wrapped logo on a tapered glass is not a flat sticker. The proof is shown on the molded part, so the approved artwork is what prints — including where it stops and how far the color runs.

Packing

Bulk, sleeve, insert, gift box

Bulk carton, polybag, printed sleeve, gift box and foam or paper insert. Packing is chosen with the carton in mind, because the pack quantity per carton is what the freight and the unit price are built on.

What we need to quote decoration

  • Artwork as vector (AI, EPS, SVG or PDF), text converted to outlines
  • The print position and the area that must stay clear of the rim and the base radius
  • A Pantone reference for every spot color, or a clear / frosted / solid direction
  • The packing format and the quantity per carton you sell in

What comes back

  • A written note on which method fits the artwork, and which does not
  • The printable area taken from the tool drawing, not from a catalogue
  • An artwork proof on the molded part before the print run
  • The packing plan with carton quantity and carton dimensions
  • Print methods in detail
  • Color and packaging options

Development workflow

Eight steps, three approval gates

Nothing is cut before the step before it is signed

The process is deliberately slow at the start and fast at the end. Every step below ends with something you approve in writing — that is what keeps a tool from being cut twice.

  1. 01

    Brief and reference

    The use, the market, the volume and whatever exists — a photo, a sample, a drawing, or nothing yet.

    You approveWe confirm in writing what we understood before any work starts.
  2. 02

    Drawing or 3D model

    A STEP/IGES file, a dimensioned drawing, or a physical sample we measure and model.

    You approveYou approve the starting geometry.
  3. 03

    DFM review

    Wall section, draft, gate position, shrinkage, tolerance stack-up and how the part will be decorated.

    You approveYou get a written list of what is difficult and what it costs to keep.
  4. 04

    Route and quotation

    Existing mold, modification or new tool — quoted with the tooling as its own line and the production minimum stated.

    You approveYou approve the route before any steel is cut.
  5. 05

    Tool build or modification

    Machining, fitting and polishing in our own shop, with progress photographs at the milestones you ask for.

    You approveNothing moves to trial until the previous step is signed off.
  6. 06

    Trial and sample

    T1 parts are measured against the drawing, then checked for fit, surface and, where relevant, leak.

    You approveYou approve the sample or send corrections; trial rounds repeat until it is right.
  7. 07

    Artwork proof

    The print is proofed on the actual taper, with the print band and colors marked.

    You approveYou sign the proof — that document governs the print.
  8. 08

    Production and inspection

    Molding, decoration, in-process checks, AQL inspection and packing to the agreed plan.

    You approveInspection records travel with the shipment.

OEM / ODM FAQ

Before the first call

What is the difference between OEM and ODM for a shot glass?

OEM means the glass is already defined — by your drawing, your sample or a written specification — and the job is to mold it exactly and document that it meets the spec. ODM means the glass still has to be defined: we develop the shape, wall section, capacity and decoration with you. In both cases the mold route (existing, modified or new) is decided separately from the label.

Can you work from a photo of a glass we like?

A photo starts the conversation but does not define a tool. We need a dimensioned drawing, a 3D file or a physical sample we can measure. With a photo alone we can tell you which route is likely and what information is missing.

Do we own the mold?

Ownership is written into the project agreement and it is one of the first things to settle. Say what you need before the tooling quotation is issued, so the agreement reflects it rather than being renegotiated afterwards.

What does a shot glass mold cost?

It is quoted per drawing. Number of tool sets, cavity count, steel grade, slides and trial rounds are separate lines, so you can see what removing a feature would save. It is not a per-kilo or per-cup figure, and we do not publish a price band that would not survive the first drawing review.

How long from drawing to production?

The window is quoted against the tool and the decoration plan rather than promised on a page. The drivers are known: a single-cavity body is quicker than a multi-cavity tool with slides, and trial rounds add time. Publish-quality dates are confirmed in the quotation after the drawing review.

Can you keep our design confidential?

Confidentiality terms go into the project agreement before drawings are exchanged. Say so in your first message and the exchange is handled under those terms from the start — including what happens to tooling, samples and rejected parts.

Do you mold silicone shot glasses as well?

We mold silicone, but a silicone shot glass is a different product family with a different process. It is quoted, sampled and documented separately from the plastic range, and it is not mixed into a plastic project.

Which documents can you supply for a project?

Material and migration reports for the exact resin and color used, plus inspection records for the run. Which documents you actually need depends on the market you sell into, so list them in the enquiry instead of assuming a standard pack covers it.

Can you handle labels, barcodes and marketplace packing?

Yes. Carton marks, barcode labels and marketplace prep are part of the packing plan and are confirmed before production, because they change the carton and sometimes the unit pack.
