FIRST, THE DECISION

Most branded cups do not need a new mould.

Three different things get called “custom”. Only one of them requires tooling, and the difference decides both the cost and the minimum order.

Use our existing mould

Pick a model from the library and change colour, print, finish or packing. No tooling cost and the lowest minimum — 10 pieces for plain or one-colour print, 250 for multi-colour or a matched body colour.

Model-specific MOQ · free design and product mockup

Change one part

A new lid, a new insert, a different rim or a custom base on a body we already mould. One small mould instead of a full set, so the commitment stays small.

1 mould · from 1,000 pcs

Cut a new product

A body or structure that does not exist yet. 2–4 mould sets, dimensional review, trial rounds, then a production minimum of 3,000 pieces. The tooling is quoted as its own line, not folded into the unit price.

2–4 moulds · 3,000 pcs

If a customer brings a drawing of something we can already make, we say so. Quoting a new mould for an existing shape is the fastest way to lose a repeat account.

WHAT MOVES THE NUMBER

Five things decide what a mould costs — not the size of the cup.

Tooling is quoted per drawing, because two cups that look alike can differ threefold once you open the drawing. These are the five drivers, and what they do to your cost and your MOQ.

DriverWhat it changesEffect on costEffect on MOQ
Number of mould setsA body, a lid, a straw, a base — each separate part needs its own toolingHigh — the main multiplierRises with each part
Cavity count1 cavity for a slow, thick part; 4–8 cavities for a high-volume thin-wall cupHigher upfront, lower unit costHigher, because output must absorb it
Steel and hardnessP20 for tens of thousands of shots; hardened steel for a programme of millionsMediumHigher for hardened tooling
Slides, unscrews and threadsUndercuts, handles, threaded lids and hollow bases need side actionsHigh — often the surprise lineHigher, tooling is more fragile
Surface and textureEtched grain, high-polish optical surfaces, in-mould labellingMedium to highNeutral
Can you tell me the tooling cost from a photo?

Not reliably. A photo can tell us the family the product belongs to and roughly how many parts it has, which is enough for a budget range. The firm number comes after a dimensioned drawing or a 3D file.

Is the mould mine or yours?

Agreed at quotation. Either the tooling is charged to you and stays yours for the life of the programme, or it is amortised into the unit price. Which one is used, and who holds the tool, is written into the order — never left verbal.

What if the first trial parts are not right?

Trial rounds are part of the tooling, not an extra. Two to three rounds are typical: T1 checks dimensions and fill, T2 fixes what the first round showed, T3 is the sample we send for approval. Extra rounds caused by a design change after T1 are quoted separately.

THE PATH

Six steps from a drawing to a moulded part.

Each step ends with something you approve. Nothing is cut until the previous step is signed off, because steel is faster to agree with than to change.

01

Drawing and use review

You send a 3D file, a dimensioned drawing or a sample to copy. We check wall thickness, draft, gate position, shrinkage and how the part will be decorated.

02

DFM feedback

A written review of what will be difficult: thin sections, sharp internal corners, undercuts, tolerance stack-up between lid and body. This is where cost is still negotiable.

03

Tooling quotation

Number of sets, cavity count, steel, slides, trial rounds and the production MOQ behind the tool — stated as separate lines, so you can see what removing a feature would save.

04

Mould making

Machining, fitting and polishing in our own shop, 25–35 days. Progress photographs at the milestones you ask for.

05

Trial and sample approval

T1 parts are measured against the drawing and sent to you. Dimensions, fit, leak performance and surface are checked before the tool is accepted.

06

Mass production

Sample and production schedules follow the selected product, artwork approval and project quotation.

WHAT WE NEED TO QUOTE

What to send, so the tooling number is real.

Missing one of these usually turns a firm quote into a range, and a range is not something a buyer can take to their manager.

01

A 3D file or dimensioned drawing

STEP, IGES or a drawing with wall thickness, capacity, height and thread details. A physical sample is also fine — we measure it.

02

Material and expected use

PP, AS, PC, Tritan or silicone, hot or cold drink, single use or a programme of several seasons.

03

Tolerance-critical features

Anything that must fit another part — a lid thread, a stackable rim, a machine interface — with the tolerance you can accept.

04

Decoration plan

Print area, wrap or 360°, laser, label or in-mould label. Decoration changes the surface the mould must produce.

05

Annual volume

First order and likely annual repeat. This decides cavity count, and cavity count decides the unit price.

06

Target unit price

If you have one, say it. It is faster to redesign a wall section than to argue about a number after the tool is cut.

We quote tooling as a separate line from the product. A quotation that folds tooling into the piece price hides the decision, and makes it impossible to compare two suppliers fairly.
