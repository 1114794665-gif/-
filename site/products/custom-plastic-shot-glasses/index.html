Material options

Four resins, four different glasses

All molded in house · no glass, no metal

01 / PP

Polypropylene

Translucent to opaque with a slightly waxy surface, and the toughest of the four at a thin wall. The usual choice for high-volume promotional shots where the glass will be dropped, stacked and dropped again.

  • Best impact resistance
  • Reads translucent, not glass-clear
  • Good for solid colors and one-color print

If clarity is the point of the design, PP is the wrong resin.

02 / PS

Polystyrene

Hard, glossy and the clearest of the commodity resins. It reads closest to glass on a bar top and takes a sharp print, which is why single-use shots are so often PS.

  • Closest glass look at low cost
  • More brittle than PP under impact
  • Confirm contact time with high-strength spirits

Ask about crazing before specifying PS for a high-ABV product.

03 / AS (SAN)

Styrene acrylonitrile

Clearer and stiffer than PS with better resistance to stress crazing. Reusable bar programmes tend to land here: it looks like glass, survives a wash, and holds a straight wall without going milky.

  • Better for repeated washing than PS
  • Sharper edge definition at the rim
  • Print adhesion needs the right pre-treatment

The middle ground when the glass must look good more than once.

04 / Tritan

Tritan copolyester

Crystal clear, tough, and the grade most often specified when the glass has to survive a commercial wash cycle without clouding. It costs more per piece than the three above.

  • Clarity that holds up over time
  • Best choice for a reusable programme
  • Grade documentation comes from the resin supplier

Washing limits are confirmed per model, not per resin.

We mold plastic and silicone. We do not mold glass, ceramic or metal, so a brief that needs those is out of scope — and we will say so at the first review rather than at the sample.

Capacity and size

The cavity decides the pour

Capacity is not a choice you make on a page — it is the volume between the core and the cavity. So there are only two ways to arrive at it: match a tool that already exists, or define a new one around the pour you actually serve.

Give the pour
A “single shot” is 25, 30, 40, 44 or 50 ml depending on the market you sell into. Quote the pour in the unit your buyer uses, not in “small” or “large”.
Choose the rim
A straight rim and a rolled rim give different finished heights from the same body, and a rolled rim hides a thin wall better.
Choose the base
Solid, recessed or ribbed changes how the glass sits on a tray, how it stacks, and how much resin the part uses.
Check the fit
If the glass has to nest in a rack, a tray or a cup holder, say so before the tool is chosen — not after the sample arrives.
We do not publish a capacity range. Two glasses that look alike can differ by 15 ml, and that difference is the gap between a legal measure and a short pour.

Mold development options

Three routes. Three different commitments.

Route terms per our published tooling page

Pick the route by what you want to change, not by what the project is called. A glass that only needs a new logo does not need a new tool, and a glass that needs 10 ml more capacity usually does.

01 / EXISTING MOLD

Existing mold production

Run a tool that already exists

Best for: a launch date that cannot move

What changes
Color, logo, decoration, packing
What stays
The tool, the dimensions, the wall section

The fastest route and the cheapest per piece, because the tool is already paid for. You cannot change capacity or shape here — only how the glass looks.

CommitmentExisting-tool minimumNew tooling: no

02 / MODIFY A MOLD

Existing mold modification

Change one part of a tool

Best for: a glass that is close but not right

What changes
Capacity, rim, base, wall section, logo area
What stays
The tool frame and the outside envelope

One insert instead of a full tool. This is where a glass gains 10 ml of capacity or loses a heavy base without paying for a new mold.

CommitmentOne-part modification minimumNew tooling: the changed insert only

03 / NEW MOLD

New mold development

Cut a new tool

Best for: a shape that does not exist yet

What changes
Everything
What stays
Nothing

Two to four tool sets depending on the design, a dimensional review, trial rounds, then production on the approved tool. Tooling is quoted as its own line.

CommitmentNew-tool production minimumNew tooling: required

Route terms follow the published tooling routes: an existing tool carries the lowest minimum, a one-part modification sits in the middle, a new tool carries a production minimum because the tool is paid for by the run. For a shot glass the exact figure is confirmed in writing after the drawing review — see Custom tooling and mold cost.

What the artwork has to be

  • Vector artwork: AI, EPS, SVG or PDF
  • Drawn 1:1 with a dimension on it, so the print band can be checked
  • A Pantone reference for every spot color
  • Text converted to outlines
  • Critical detail kept away from the rim and the base radius

What the proof settles

A wrapped logo on a tapered glass is not a flat sticker. The print is proofed on the actual taper, so what you approve is what prints — including where the artwork stops, how far the color runs and whether the mark clears the rim.

Process visuals are illustrative; the artwork proof for your project is the document that governs the print.

Color

Color is approved on a molded part

Color comes from masterbatch matched to a Pantone reference. Clear, frosted, translucent and solid all read differently once the wall thickness changes, so the swatch that looks right on paper is not the approval.

  • Clear
  • Frosted
  • Opaque white
  • Black
  • Brand clay
  • Amber
  • Sage

The same chip reads differently on PP and on AS, and a frosted surface shifts it again. Approve on a molded sample, keep that sample, and the next run has something to match.

Packaging

The box is part of the product

Packing is decided before production, not after: the pack quantity changes the carton, the carton changes the freight, and a retail box changes how the glass has to be protected inside it.

Bulk carton
The cheapest per piece; the default for bar and event supply.
Polybag or sleeve
A printed sleeve gives a retail read without a full box.
Gift box and insert
The insert is designed around the glass, so it is settled with the sample.
Labels and barcodes
Barcode, carton marks and marketplace prep are added at packing, per order.

Application scenarios

What changes the specification

Six channels, six different first questions

Bars & nightclubs

Breakage is the cost line

Choose the resin for impact first, then the print for wash and abrasion resistance. A glass that shatters on the third night is not cheap.

Distilleries & brand promo

The glass carries the label

Match the brand color on a molded part, keep the print band clear of the rim, and settle the packaging with the sample.

Events & catering

Volume and packing speed

Bulk cartons that open fast and glass that stacks on a tray matter more than piece weight. Say how the venue unpacks.

Hotels & hospitality

Repeated washing

Confirm the wash cycle and the detergent before choosing the resin — that decision fixes AS or Tritan, not the logo.

Retail gift sets

The box is half the product

Design the insert around the glass, then confirm the glass. Doing it the other way round means re-tooling the insert.

Sampling & trade shows

Small quantities, one mark

Trial quantities are possible on an existing tool, with a mark that survives being handled all day.

Product FAQ

Questions that come before a sample

Can you mold shot glasses to my drawing?

Yes. Send a STEP or IGES file, or a dimensioned drawing, and we return a DFM review covering wall section, draft, gate position, shrinkage and how the part will be decorated. The tooling quote follows that review — not the other way round.

What is the minimum order for custom plastic shot glasses?

It follows the mold route, not the product. A tool that already exists carries the lowest minimum, a one-part modification sits in the middle, and a new tool carries a production minimum because the tool has to be paid for by the run. Our published route terms apply, and the shot-glass figure is confirmed in writing with the quotation.

Do you have existing shot glass molds we can use?

We hold tooling across the drinkware range and the list changes as projects finish. Send the pour and the look you need and we check what is available on an existing tool before anyone discusses cutting a new one. If a new mold is not needed, we say so.

Which plastic should I choose for a shot glass?

PP for impact resistance and volume, PS for the closest glass look at low cost, AS for a clearer reusable bar glass, Tritan where the glass has to survive repeated washing. The grade is named on the drawing, and the food-contact documents follow that grade.

Can you print a full wrap on a tapered glass?

Yes, up to the printable band the tool allows. The proof is produced on the actual taper so the artwork does not stretch or drift towards the rim. The number of colors per pass is confirmed with that proof.

Will the glass taste or smell of plastic?

That depends on the resin grade and how the part is molded, not on the word plastic. Ask for the migration documents for the grade and color you are ordering, and keep the first-article sample from the trial run as your reference for the next order.

Are they dishwasher safe?

Washing limits are model-specific: resin, wall section and print method all matter. Tell us the wash cycle the glass has to survive and we confirm it against the grade instead of promising it for the whole range.

Can you match my brand color?

Yes, by masterbatch against a Pantone reference. Approve the color on a molded part rather than on a paper chip — the same chip reads differently on PP and on AS, and the surface finish changes it again.

Do you supply food-contact reports?

For the exact resin and color used, issued by a third-party laboratory. The documents follow the material that is actually molded, so they are requested per order rather than published per page.

How long does production take?

It depends on the route and is quoted against your project rather than promised on a page: a tool that already exists moves faster than one that has to be cut. The schedule is confirmed with the quotation, after the drawing or model has been reviewed.
